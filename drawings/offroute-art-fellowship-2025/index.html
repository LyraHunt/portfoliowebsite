Offroute Art Fellowship

(2025)

Local art fellowship that I created three final pieces for.

Offroute Art is a non-profit organization that supports young artists in Pittsburgh, PA. I applied to and attended their Fall 2025 Art Fellowship along with 7 other artists. We had monthly in-person meetings in which we discussed personal, social, and political conflicts and how they present themselves in our communities. Over the course of the meetings the topic of empathy came up frequently, and we decided on "Crisis of Empathy/Lack of Empathy" as our conflict theme for the final showing.

My three final pieces and their descriptions are listed below:

I'm Not My Season

Personal/Relational Empathy

This piece engages us with a large pair of eyes, while a wooden mannequin figure hangs in the air and obstructs our view. The figure is objectified, its face is turned away from us and it seems powerless in front of the gaze. Below, telephone poles create a tangle of disconnected wires. These subjects grab our attention in conflicting ways. Do you make eye contact while ignoring the mannequin? Do you project onto the figure, and feel the eyes looking past you? Is the mannequin falling or floating?

I have long been a fan of the band Fleet Foxes. Their songs discuss empathy and relationships, and they use eyes as a metaphor for connection. Their music has inspired this body of work as a whole, and this piece was inspired by this verse from Mearcstapa in particular:

Two lines in the air
Two eyes on the pair
Mearcstapa, on an open sea
But you turn away
No falling today
No wind in the night
You're putting slack in the lines

Newgrowth

Environmental Empathy

This piece depicts a landscape that transitions from organic and tangible on the left, to artificial and theoretical on the right. I am constantly thinking about the line between the natural and artificial in my work, and I don't believe that there is a difference between these worlds. Nature creates structures that seem artificial yet nature is integral to our being; we can't escape it or define a line between us. Do you connect more with one side or the other? Do you feel that these sides coexist?

There is a human figure in the center that is disconnected from the landscape behind it. This out-of-context figure represents our tendency to center ourselves and simultaneously view ourselves as separate from the natural. If we believe that we are separate then we are no longer grounded. This extends to climate change, which is directly the result of our industry and colonization and yet we are affected by it just as everything else is.

Fleet Foxes, A long way past the past:
Oh man, was it that much better then?
We were left alone, we were proud of our pain
And so I want to walk out in the night
See the wide young river flood rain

Eyes On The Capital

Social/Political Empathy

This piece depicts sterile dome structures: authority and power. The structure is faceless, it is vaguely utopian but sinister at the same time. Behind the structure, I used a sharpie to create a claustrophobic background and a single, large eye that is watching from behind the facade. The eye represents the ever-growing presence of surveillance in today's society. This world may look powerful, but it is certainly not the face of community.

This constant watching and being watched takes a toll on the individual, whether it's spending hours scrolling the news or arguing on social media. Authority seems untouchable as they further divide us by attacking our empathy and critical thinking, but this piece invites us to consider what power we have of our own. The more we can support each other and build community the less control they have, and the stronger we are.
Empathy is resistance.

Fleet Foxes, If You Need To, Keep Time on Me:
How could it all fall in one day?
Were we too sure of the sun?

If you need to, keep time on me
